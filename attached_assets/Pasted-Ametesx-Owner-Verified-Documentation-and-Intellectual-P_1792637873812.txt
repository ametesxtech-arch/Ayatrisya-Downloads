Ametesx — Owner-Verified Documentation and Intellectual Property Register

Ayatrisya is an Ametesx product. Build on the existing documentation implementation; do not create duplicate pages or invent legal facts.

1. Audit all existing documents

Inspect the public documentation pages, internal IP inventory, repository licence files, package manifests, contributor records, privacy-related configuration, model licences, third-party notices and deployment configuration.

Create an internal register with:

* Document name and location
* Public or internal status
* Source of each material claim
* Whether the claim is owner-confirmed, implementation-verified, externally sourced, or awaiting approval
* Outstanding evidence and required owner action
* Last reviewed date, without inventing a review date

2. Protect Ametesx’s original work

Document the intended ownership of eligible original Ametesx work, including original source code, website content, product designs, documentation, graphics and original model code.

For each asset, distinguish:

* Created directly by the owner
* Created by a contributor or contractor
* Generated using an external platform or tool
* Derived from or bundled with third-party material
* Ownership or licence not yet established

Do not mark an asset as legally owned by Ametesx solely because it appears in an Ametesx product.

3. Check licensing conflicts

Inspect the actual root MIT licence and all relevant package-level licence declarations. Do not assume that the MIT licence applies only to dependencies or only to application code.

Identify conflicting or unclear licence statements. Preserve mandatory third-party notices and model, dataset, font, icon and library licence terms. Do not relicense material without the necessary authority.

4. Legal documents

Keep the Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Copyright/IP page, Licences and Cookies page accessible through stable routes. Where a document is not approved, show a professional draft-status notice rather than a broken page or unsupported legal claims.

Keep missing legal operator information, verified contact details, jurisdiction, endpoint-retention terms and rights provenance as explicit owner-review items. Do not invent them.

5. Owner approval workflow

Provide a private internal checklist that allows the owner to review and confirm factual statements. Owner confirmation must not be treated as proof of a third-party assignment, licence permission, company registration or legal transfer that has not actually occurred.

Do not expose private notes, credentials, internal file paths or sensitive deployment information on public pages.

6. Final verification

Test the public documentation routes, footer links, responsive layouts, typecheck, lint, existing tests and production build. Report exact results and outstanding blockers.

Do not label the entire documentation set “owner-verified” unless every included claim has a traceable basis and the necessary approvals have actually been obtained.
